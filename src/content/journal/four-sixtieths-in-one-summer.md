Six frames from the summer of 2024 on the Galaxy S24 Ultra are at exactly 1/60s. Two are on
the wide cameras, where 1/60s is nothing. The other four are on the telephotos, three at
115mm and one at 69mm, and those four are the interesting ones, because three of them are
animals.

[*Gaze of the Urban Stalker*](/photo/gaze-of-the-urban-stalker) is a tabby cat stretched
out on tarmac, front paws extended, head lifted to look up past the camera. 115mm, f/3.4,
1/60s at ISO 100, from late June.

[*Crimson and Caliber*](/photo/crimson-and-caliber) is a few days later: a red cardinal and
a brown sparrow at opposite ends of a string of cafe lights, a weathered timber post between
them. Same 115mm, same 1/60s, same ISO 100. The cardinal is the only saturated thing in the
frame, and it is sitting still.

[*A Quiet Feeding*](/photo/a-quiet-feeding) is August, an adult house sparrow feeding a young
one beak to beak on a wooden ledge with seed scattered around them and string lights hanging
behind. This one is on the 3x lens at 69mm and f/2.4, 1/60s at ISO 80.

[*Crimson Ascent*](/photo/crimson-ascent) is the end of the month and not an animal at all.
A young woman in a full red ball gown and tiara climbing wide stone steps, another woman in
a red sequinned dress waiting above her with a bouquet, and the trees behind taken down to
grey so the red is the only colour left. 115mm, 1/60s, ISO 100 again.

The old rule of thumb says the slowest safe shutter speed is one over the focal length,
which at 115mm is about 1/125s. The three at 115mm are a stop under that, on subjects that
can move whenever they like. Stabilisation covers a shaking hand, and it does nothing for a
sparrow that decides to lean forward. The file does not record whether 1/60s was my choice
or the phone's, but four times running in one summer it is where the exposure landed. The
Canon version of the same argument, at 200mm and at night, is in [A Sixtieth at Two
Hundred](/journal/a-sixtieth-at-two-hundred).

The same 115mm lens appears once more, months earlier and in very different light.
[*Midnight Downpour*](/photo/midnight-downpour) is heavy rain falling in hard streaks through
the glare of a single street lamp, bare branches behind it, in black and white. March 2024,
1/25s at ISO 2500. That is about six stops more exposure than the summer frames were given
on the same lens, and the rain is streaked because at 1/25s it had time to fall through the
frame. Here the slow shutter is the picture.
