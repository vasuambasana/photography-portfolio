There are twenty-six photographs in the architecture category here. Twenty-four of them were
taken on a phone. One has no camera recorded at all. One came off the Canon.

That is not a position I hold about gear. It is what happens when the subject does not move
and you are walking past it anyway.

Buildings are the most forgiving thing to photograph. They stay still, they are usually
outdoors, and they are frequently in full sun. Seventeen of these frames sit at ISO 50 or
lower, and three of them are at ISO 12. A sensor reporting ISO 12 is a sensor with more
light than it can use. [*Mirrored Sky*](/photo/mirrored-sky) is one of those, a glass tower
shot from its base with blue sky and cloud running down the face of it, 23mm equivalent at
f/1.8 and 1/750s. [*Glass Among the Brick*](/photo/glass-among-the-brick) is another, ISO 12
at 1/1150s, a dark tower standing over low brick streets from above.

None of that needed a camera. It needed being there when the light was doing it.

The focal lengths are the part I did not expect. I assumed a set like this would live at the
wide end, because that is what you reach for standing under something tall, and there is
plenty of that here at 23mm. But two of the strongest frames are at 230mm equivalent on a
phone's super-telephoto.
[*Urban Symmetry: Boston's Transit Axis*](/photo/urban-symmetry-boston-s-transit-axis) is
one of them, an avenue running dead to the horizon with tracks down its median and a Citgo
sign on a rooftop at the right, compressed until the whole city looks stacked. The other is
[*Silent Ascent*](/photo/silent-ascent), a church spire against blank overcast with one bird
in the frame, at 1/1500s and ISO 40.

Compression like that used to mean carrying something heavy.
[*Monolith in Monochrome*](/photo/monolith-in-monochrome) sits between the two ends at 69mm
equivalent, the top of the Prudential Tower with bare winter branches across the front of
it, at ISO 50. [*Steel and Sky*](/photo/steel-and-sky) is back at 23mm and ISO 16, a grid of
glass against a deep blue sky with branches catching the sun at the edge.

Then there is the exception. The single Canon frame in the category is
[*Symphony in Steel and Shadow*](/photo/symphony-in-steel-and-shadow), from a January night,
at **ISO 5000**. It is the highest ISO anywhere in this archive. The bridge tower is almost
entirely black and the cables fan out of the dark as thin pale lines.

Which is the actual pattern, once you line the numbers up. The phone covers buildings in
daylight completely and there is nothing left for a bigger sensor to do. The one time the
Canon came out for a building, it was because there was almost no light at all, and that is
still the only thing that reliably separates them.
