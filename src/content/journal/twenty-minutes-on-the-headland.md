One frame survives from this afternoon, and it is worth saying up front that it was taken on
a Galaxy S24 Ultra.

[*Winter on the Headland*](/photo/winter-on-the-headland) is 23mm equivalent, f/1.7, 1/180s,
at ISO 20. Low sun on an icy rocky shoreline, evergreens above the water, a lighthouse
standing out along the point. Late afternoon in the middle of February on that coast, which
is about as late as the light gets.

ISO 20 is the part that tells you what actually happened. A phone only drops that low when
it has light to burn, and a flat grey overcast does not produce it. So the sky must have
broken somewhere near the horizon, because the sensor is reporting direct sun on the rock
while the frame still reads as a winter afternoon.

That is the whole appeal of going out there in February. In summer the same headland is
crowded and the light is hazy and the sunset turns to mush. In winter the place is empty and
the light, when it arrives at all, is hard and short.

The other coast frame from that winter is [*Winter Stroll by the
Shore*](/photo/winter-stroll-by-the-shore), a month earlier in January, someone walking a dog
across snow at the waterline. That one is a different day and a different phone, a Galaxy S22
Ultra at 230mm, and it is here because it is the same stretch of coast in the same cold
rather than because it came from the same afternoon.

I would like to tell you what the temperature was. I do not have it, and the file does not
record it, so I am not going to make one up.
