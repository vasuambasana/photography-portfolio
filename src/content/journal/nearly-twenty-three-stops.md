Seven frames in this archive carry the date 23 April 2023. Two of them sit further apart in
exposure than any other pair taken on the same day, and they were made on different phones.

[*Cosmic Tapestry Over Silhouetted Peaks*](/photo/cosmic-tapestry-over-silhouetted-peaks)
is the Milky Way core low over a dark ridge, a band of airglow along the horizon. Galaxy S22
Ultra, twenty seconds at f/1.8 and ISO 1600. The focal length reads 6.4mm rather than 23mm,
which is the same main camera recording its real focal length instead of the 35mm
equivalent. The two night frames from that trip are the only ones here that do.

[*Solitude Beneath the Endless Blue*](/photo/solitude-beneath-the-endless-blue) is a person
standing alone on the white polygons of a salt flat with snow on the mountains behind them,
at 1/7500s, f/2.2 and ISO 50. It is the only photograph in this archive from a Galaxy S23
Ultra. Every other frame from those days came off the S22.

Put the two exposures on the same footing and the night frame received 22.8 stops more light
than the salt flat. That is a factor of roughly seven million. Nothing about it was
difficult. One phone had to be held still for twenty seconds, the other was pointed at a
white salt crust under a deep blue sky, and each camera did the arithmetic for itself.

The night before has the same settings exactly.
[*Highway to the Cosmos*](/photo/highway-to-the-cosmos), dated 22 April, is a two-lane road
running away under a sky dense with stars, the double yellow line leading toward low hills.
Twenty seconds, f/1.8, ISO 1600 again. Two nights, identical numbers.

The daylight frames on that date sit between those extremes, and much closer to the bright
one. [*Granite and Winter Light*](/photo/granite-and-winter-light) is a snow-covered granite
peak pulled in at 230mm equivalent, 1/350s at ISO 40.
[*Vista of the High Desert*](/photo/vista-of-the-high-desert) is someone in a backwards cap
sitting on a boulder looking out over a basin of scattered rock toward a snowy range, on the
ultrawide at 1/1500s and ISO 50. Both are within two and a half stops of the salt flat.

The other frames from the same date are in [Finding Scale in Death
Valley](/journal/finding-scale-in-death-valley), and they are daylight too. So the day is a
spread of daylight exposures about five stops wide, and then one frame at least seventeen
stops below every one of them, made in the dark hours of the same date.

That spread is the reason phones have a night mode at all. There is no single exposure
that suits both ends of it, and I did not have to choose one. Two phones covered
twenty-three stops between them and all I did was point them.
