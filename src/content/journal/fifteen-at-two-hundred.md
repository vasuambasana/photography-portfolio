The RF 70-200mm f/2.8 is the most used lens in this archive by a distance. Thirty-three
frames, which is about a quarter of everything here, and far and away the most expensive
object involved.

Going through the focal lengths, it is barely being used as a zoom at all. Fifteen frames
are at exactly 200mm. Eight are at exactly 70mm. That leaves ten photographs spread across
the entire range in between, and four of those are at 135mm.

So twenty-three of thirty-three sit at one end or the other. I did not know that until I
counted, and I would have told you before counting that I worked the zoom constantly.

What is actually happening is that the two ends do different jobs and I switch between jobs
rather than between focal lengths.

Two hundred is for not being involved.
[*A Quiet Encounter in the Green*](/photo/a-quiet-encounter-in-the-green) is a squirrel on a
tree root, sharp, with a woman sitting on the grass behind it dissolved into nothing. At
f/2.8 and that reach, the background is gone as a place and survives only as colour.
[*Shared Horizons*](/photo/shared-horizons) is a woman and a girl leaning on a railing,
heads down, at 1/2500s, and neither of them had any reason to notice a camera existed.
[*Reflections by the Waterside*](/photo/reflections-by-the-waterside) is the same distance
applied to a couple and a small child at the edge of a pond.

Seventy is for the opposite, when the setting is half the photograph.
[*Spring's Velvet Tapestry*](/photo/spring-s-velvet-tapestry) is a magnolia in full bloom
filling the frame at f/5, where the point is the sheer quantity of flowers rather than any
one of them. [*Afloat in the Festival*](/photo/afloat-in-the-festival) is two decorative
balloon spinners held sharp with a whole field of real balloons and marquees going soft
behind them, which only works because 70mm keeps the field in the picture.

The ten frames in the middle are the ones I find hardest to explain.
[*Solitude in the Shade*](/photo/solitude-in-the-shade) at 135mm is a person reading against
a tree trunk with the park still legible around them, and
[*A Tower of Love*](/photo/a-tower-of-love), also 135mm, is a boy on a woman's shoulders in
a crowd. Both of those are compromises in the good sense. Close enough to see a face,
far enough that the place is still there.

None of which is an argument for a zoom or against one. It is just that the thing I thought
I was buying, a continuous range, is not the thing the metadata says I use. I use a 70 and a
200 that happen to share a barrel, and the ten frames in between are the ones where I
actually stopped to think about it.
