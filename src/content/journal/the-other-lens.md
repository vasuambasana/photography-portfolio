The other Canon lens in this archive is an RF 35mm f/1.8 Macro, and it has six frames to the
70-200's thirty-three.

Five of the six are at f/1.8. Not near it, at it. The lens gets put on and opened all the way
and left there, which tells you it is not being chosen for depth of field so much as for
being small and fast and close.

[*Coastal Quietude*](/photo/coastal-quietude) is the extreme case, f/1.8 at 1/8000s,
which is the fastest shutter speed anywhere on this site. A child crouching at the edge of a
calm inlet with kayaks stacked on the sand beyond. Nothing in that frame needed 1/8000s.
It is simply what f/1.8 costs when you point a camera at bright water at ISO 200, and the
shutter runs out of room before the aperture does.

The same thing happens with [*Unbound Spirit*](/photo/unbound-spirit) at 1/4000s, a small
child running across grass with both arms out, and
[*Playing with the Past*](/photo/playing-with-the-past) at 1/3200s, a girl with a hula hoop
standing beside a bronze statue of a child.

Those two are worth putting next to the 200mm frames, because they are the same subject
matter treated the opposite way. At 200mm a stranger has no idea you are there. At 35mm you
are close enough to be part of what is happening, and both of those photographs only exist
because being noticed did not matter.

[*A Sunny Day in the Park*](/photo/a-sunny-day-in-the-park) and
[*Grounded in Decay*](/photo/grounded-in-decay) round out the daylight five, at 1/2000s and
1/1000s.

The sixth is the interesting one, because it breaks every part of the pattern.
[*Light Through Sandstone*](/photo/light-through-sandstone) is the only 35mm frame not shot
wide open, at f/2.8, and the only one in the dark, at 1/60s and ISO 1600. Smooth orange slot
canyon walls with purple in the shadows. Stopping down to f/2.8 inside a slot canyon is a
strange choice when light is that scarce, and I suspect it was about getting the near and
far wall both acceptably sharp rather than about anything else.

Six frames is not much of a case for a lens. What it does show is that the two Canon lenses
here are not points on a continuum, they are two different relationships with the subject,
and the 35mm only comes out when I have already decided to be near enough to be seen.
