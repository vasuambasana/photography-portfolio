Going back through the architecture I found the same picture at least five times. A building
comes in from one side of the frame, stops at a hard edge, and the rest of the rectangle is
sky. Not the building in its setting, not the street, just the meeting line between the two.

The oldest is [*Amber Reflections on Concrete*](/photo/amber-reflections-on-concrete), from
November 2021 on a OnePlus 7 Pro: the corner of a glass-clad tower, its windows lit orange,
against blue sky and cloud underlit in amber. 74mm equivalent, f/2.4, 1/480s at ISO 100.

[*Pixels and Panes*](/photo/pixels-and-panes) is March 2023 on a Galaxy S22 Ultra, the
corner where a facade of small blue and green mosaic tiles meets a plain grey glass wall,
under scattered cloud. 69mm, 1/1300s, ISO 40.
[*Celestial Blanket*](/photo/celestial-blanket) is the same phone and the same 69mm in
November: the eaves of a clapboard house seen from below against a sky entirely covered in
fine rippled cloud, bare branches and rust-coloured leaves crowding the bottom. 1/2000s at
ISO 40.

[*Crimson Horizon*](/photo/crimson-horizon) is June 2024 on an S24 Ultra at 115mm, the
rooflines of two apartment buildings against a sky of red and orange cloud, one window
reflecting the same colour back. 1/30s at ISO 400, slower than any of the others by more than
three stops.

Four of the five are on a telephoto, at 69mm, 74mm or 115mm. That is the part I had not
noticed. A long lens means standing well back, and from well back you cannot get the whole
building into the frame anyway, so you stop trying and take an edge of it. The sky fills
whatever the building does not. The result is flat, graphic, and nothing like how the
building looks when you walk past it.

The exception is [*Monolith in Monotone*](/photo/monolith-in-monotone), on the S22's main
camera at 23mm, f/1.8, 1/340s and ISO 12, and in black and white. It is a concrete tower
shot from its own base, one face smooth and the other stepped into a row of sharp
triangular fins, against churning grey cloud. Same idea reached the other way: instead of
standing far enough back to crop the building, stand so close to it that it cannot help
leaning out of the frame.

The other thing all five share is that the sky is doing something. Amber cloud, scattered
cloud, rippled cloud, grey cloud, red cloud. Not one of them is a clear blue sky, and I
think that is the real rule behind the composition: half the frame is given to the sky, so
the sky has to be worth half a frame.
