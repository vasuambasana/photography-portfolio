Only six photographs in this archive are older than 2023, and three of them came from a
Galaxy Note10+ between August and September 2022. The file only ever says SM-N976U, which is
the model code. It had to be looked up.

The three have more in common than the phone. All three are the sea. All three are at 52mm
equivalent and f/2.1, which is the Note's 2x telephoto rather than its main camera. And all
three are at ISO 50.

[*Last Light Drift*](/photo/last-light-drift) is a paddleboarder in silhouette on calm
water, the whole surface throwing back an orange sky, and a contrail cutting across the top
corner. [*Shelter Against the Tide*](/photo/shelter-against-the-tide) looks down from above
on two people wrapped together in a pink blanket at the rail of a metal stairway, dark water
churning white underneath them.
[*Golden Hour Shoreline*](/photo/golden-hour-shoreline) is also from above, a long curve of
sand with people and dogs scattered along the edge of the surf.

Two of the three look down on the water from a height rather than across it. From above, the
sea stops being a horizon and becomes a surface with a pattern on it, foam and wet sand and
the paths people leave.

With the aperture fixed and the ISO sitting on 50 in all three, the shutter speed is the
only number that moves, so it carries the entire record of the light. The sunset is 1/500s.
The two frames looking down are 1/115s and 1/100s. The fastest of the three is the one
looking straight into the sunset, which is obvious once you say it: the frames aimed down
at water and sand simply had less light coming back at them.

The gap between those shutter speeds is about two and a third stops, and that is the whole
exposure story of the Note in this archive. It was not asked to do anything difficult. It
was pointed at the sea on summer days and it got the sea.
