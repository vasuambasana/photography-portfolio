The afternoon in the middle of this is already written up in
[Twenty Minutes on the Headland](/journal/twenty-minutes-on-the-headland): one frame of low
sun on an icy shoreline with a lighthouse out on the point. What that entry does not
mention is that the same phone was out in the dark on both sides of it.

[*Midnight Canopy*](/photo/midnight-canopy) was taken at seven minutes past three in the
morning on 18 February 2024, by the capture time inside the file. Stars crowding a dark
blue sky above the tops of spruce and bare branches, the branches lit faintly from
somewhere below. I cannot tell you what the light was. The frame does not show a source.

[*Cosmic Canopy*](/photo/cosmic-canopy) was taken at twenty-three minutes past one the
following night. A field of stars with the black tops of spruce closing in from both sides
of the frame, and nothing lit at all.

Between them sits [*Winter on the Headland*](/photo/winter-on-the-headland), in the
afternoon of the 18th. So the order is stars, coast, stars, over about twenty-two hours.

Both night frames are on the Galaxy S24 Ultra's main camera at 23mm equivalent and f/1.7,
twenty seconds at ISO 1600. Identical settings, a night apart. They are also the only two
exposures that long this phone made anywhere in the archive; every other twenty- and
twenty-five-second frame here came from a different phone.

Both are pointed well up into the sky, with the treetops pushing in from the bottom and the
sides rather than standing in a row along a horizon. At twenty seconds on a wide lens the
stars are still points rather than trails, and the trees give the frame a floor to stand on.
Without them it would be a picture of the sky from anywhere. With them it is a picture of
one particular stand of trees, even if the file cannot tell me which.

The headland entry ends by admitting it does not know the temperature. Neither do these.
Two nights in mid-February, twenty seconds each time, with the phone held still for all of
it, is as much as the files say.
